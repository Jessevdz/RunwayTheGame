Runway is a racing game played on a board somebody drew over the real
world. A board can be as small as a street, and as large
as the entire world.

A **board** is a set of waypoints (real places) joined by roads you can
traverse. Most waypoints hold a **challenge**: a small, specific thing you need to do, verified
with a photograph. The first team to reach the finish wins.

## What you need to play

Runway is played outdoors, on foot, with a phone in your hand.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **People** | Up to six teams. A team is a group walking together. |
| **Per team** | At least one phone. It is the phone whose position the board sees, so it is the one that has to be with the group. |
| **On that phone** | A browser with location and camera permission granted. |
| **A board** | Somebody has to have drawn one first, see [below](#where-boards-come-from). |

## Clearing a Waypoint

You walk to a waypoint, verify your location, 
and complete the challenge by photographing your answer to unlock the next leg of your route.

<CaptureLoop client:visible />

A challenge is a small, specific task that can be settled by a single photograph.
These are written by whoever drew the board. For example:

> Eat something grape flavored: Packaging must specify grape.

A challenge you do not want to clear (e.g., it's too
slow, too silly or too difficult) can be **vetoed**. You decline it, the road
opens for you anyway, and you move on immediately. A veto always works, and it
always costs you something; what it costs depends on the game mode.

The finish waypoint is the only exception: it carries no challenge and pays no
reward. Arriving is the only objective.

## Charting a path

From the waypoint you are standing on, the only places you can go next are the waypoints directly connected to it. 
A waypoint fifty metres away is out of reach if no road connects you to it.

<ChartingPath client:visible />

Other than that, the route is entirely yours. Teams always start together but they can take different roads to the same finish.

## Coins

The **first** team to clear a challenge earns its coin reward. Teams arriving later
do not have to do the challenge again, and earn no coins.

### Power-ups

Using coins collected by clearing challenges, you can buy power-ups that help your own team or slow down other teams.

| Power-up | What it does |
| :--- | :--- |
| `nerf` | A target team cannot move or start a challenge for a while |
| `tracker_off` | Your position is withheld from the other teams for a while |
| `challenge_skip` | Bypasses a waypoint's challenge with no veto penalty |

Every price and duration comes from the game's own ruleset, fixed when the game
is created.

## The two ways past a challenge

There are two ways past a challenge.

<OpenForWhom client:visible />

| How | Opens the road for |
| :--- | :--- |
| You complete the challenge | **The whole field.** Only the coin reward distinguishes first from the rest. |
| You veto, or use a `challenge_skip` power-up | **Your team alone.** A bypass is personal. |

So clearing a hard waypoint is partly a gift to your rivals, and vetoing it is
partly a decision to keep them working while you walk on.

## The three game modes

<Steps>

1. **Team race**: Several teams play a single board. The winner is the
   team that gets to the finish first. Coins are used to buy power-ups.

2. **Solo time trial**: A single runner plays a single board. The clock is wall time
   from start to finish plus veto penalties.

3. **Solo casual**: The same walk, just for fun. Nothing is recorded and vetoes are free.

</Steps>


The price of a veto changes between modes, because a cooldown
only means something when rivals are racing the same board:

| Mode | Cost of a veto |
| :--- | :--- |
| Team race | A cooldown. You keep walking, but you cannot take on **any** challenge until it lapses. |
| Solo time trial | Time added to your recorded clock. |
| Solo casual | Nothing. |

## How challenge evidence gets graded

Every game fixes one of three grading modes.

| Mode | What happens to your photo | 
| :--- | :--- | 
| <Badge text="trust" variant="success" /> | Honour mode. The evidence always passes, but is visible to everyone at the end of the game for disputes. |
| <Badge text="host" variant="caution" /> | Evidence is held pending until the game host approves or rejects it. | 
| <Badge text="llm" variant="danger" /> | Evidence is graded against the challenge's rubric by a multimodal LLM. | 

A GPS checking gate runs in all three modes.
Teams must be standing at the waypoint before evidence can be submitted.

The game defaults to `trust`, because it is the only mode that asks nothing
of anyone outside the game.

## Where boards come from

A board consists of waypoints placed over real places, the roads drawn
between them, and the challenges written onto them.

Boards are obtained one of two ways: **draw your own** in the board editor at
`/design`, or **fork a board somebody published** from the gallery and race
that. Either way, [Design a board](design-a-board/) covers the editor.
